# Tony Buzan-style Mind Map

Base the map on the five laws taught by Tony Buzan Training: branches,
keywords, colour, pictures, and structure. Translate the hand-drawn method into
Nomad Canvas without reducing it to a conventional box-and-arrow hierarchy.

## Central Image

- Begin at the visual center with an image that expresses the subject.
- Make the central image the strongest focal point, not a title inside an
  ordinary rectangular node.
- Use multiple colors and a distinctive silhouette where practical.
- Keep editable wording outside a generated image; avoid asking an image model
  to render important labels. The topic label may sit below the image.

## Branches

- Radiate the main branches directly from the central image.
- Keep every child branch visibly connected to its parent branch.
- Prefer organic curves over rigid straight connectors and equal-sized boxes.
- Make main branches visually stronger near the center and progressively lighter
  or thinner toward outer twigs, as current Canvas capabilities permit.
- Let a branch support its keyword or image rather than extending far beyond it.

If Canvas cannot reproduce a tapered hand-drawn branch exactly, preserve the
radial connection, curvature, hierarchy, and branch color instead of fabricating
unsupported geometry.

## Keywords

- Put one keyword or one key image on each branch.
- Use short keywords that trigger associations; do not turn branches into
  sentence containers.
- Place the keyword on or immediately above its branch so they read as one unit.
- Use larger or stronger lettering for more central ideas and smaller lettering
  as detail moves outward.

## Colour

- Use color throughout the map rather than as occasional decoration.
- Give main branches distinguishable colors and carry each color family into
  its descendants.
- Use contrast and variation to emphasize associations while keeping labels
  readable.

## Pictures

- Treat pictures, symbols, and small illustrations as part of the map's
  vocabulary, not as optional decoration added at the end.
- Use a relevant image for the center and add images to important branches where
  they strengthen recall or association.
- When image generation is available, generate simple topic-specific artwork
  for selected branch illustrations where it strengthens recall. Prefer a
  transparent or visually quiet background.
- Insert a generated image only when it is available as a Canvas-accessible
  asset through the current tool schema. Otherwise use Canvas-native marks or
  symbols.

## Structure and Associations

- Organize ideas as a radiant hierarchy: center, main branches, then connected
  sub-branches.
- Keep related material on the same branch family and distribute branches so
  the whole map remains balanced and readable.
- Use arrows, symbols, codes, or boundary-like emphasis for meaningful
  associations that cross the tree.
- Do not impose an arbitrary branch count or depth; let the subject determine
  the structure while keeping the whole image legible.

## Final Check

- The subject is represented by a central image.
- Main branches radiate from and connect to the center.
- Descendant branches remain connected and show hierarchy.
- Each branch carries one keyword or key image rather than a sentence.
- Color and pictures are used throughout with meaningful association.
- The result reads as one organic image rather than an org chart.
- Required user information remains present and unrelated Canvas content is
  unchanged.
